# 10 — 12/24/36-Month Financial Scenarios, Founder Time, and Kill Criteria

**Prepared:** 2026-11-05 · Phase 7 memo. **All revenue inputs are assumptions [A]**, calibrated to Phases 3–5 and the demand ceiling in Memo 08. Founder time is unpaid and excluded from cash figures.

## Bottom line
- **Path A (manual + community collection, no automated calling) dominates Path B (adding AI calling) in every case modeled.** Calling adds counsel cost and monthly spend, but it doesn't raise revenue unless a B2B buyer specifically needs coverage that manual and community methods can't deliver. **Don't fund calling to build a consumer audience.**
- **Everything depends on B2B.** In the base case, one B2B customer at about $750/month starting in month 9 (plus one more about every 9 months) turns Path A cash-positive cumulatively during year 2. **With no B2B customer (the low case), every path loses money indefinitely**, roughly $3.5–4k/year for Path A. That is affordable as a side project, but it is a hobby, not a business.
- **Path C (three metros by month 18) is the only route to a credible acquisition story** (Phase 3 §9 milestones). It needs ~20–30 hours a week of founder time and should start only after Chicago passes its gates.

**Reading it:**
- **Cash runway is not the constraint.** The worst case (Path C, low) loses about $61k over 3 years, and Path A's low case only about $11k. **Founder time and opportunity cost are the real investment.** At 12 hours a week for 3 years, that is about 1,900 hours.
- **The B2B assumption carries the base case.** Without the month-9 customer, base becomes low.
- **Consumer revenue alone stays tiny:** about $200/month at the base Chicago ceiling (4,000 MAU × $0.05), or about $440/month with three metros. That is consistent with Memo 08's demand ceiling and Phase 3's conclusion.

## 4. Kill, pivot, and continue criteria (pre-committed; supersede BUSINESS_RESEARCH §8.2 where they conflict)

| Checkpoint | Continue if… | Pivot if… | Stop if… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Month 2** (after manual pilot) | p90–p10 spread ≥ $1.50 in Lake View/Lincoln Park | Spread $1.00–1.50 → focus on the cross-border index (Chicago vs. Indiana) and B2B | Spread < $1.00 **and** no cross-border gap worth reporting |
| **Month 6** | ≥3 serious B2B conversations, ≥1 paid pilot or LOI; search impressions growing | Consumer traction but no B2B → keep as a low-cost side project (Path A, minimal spend) | No B2B interest **and** <1% penetration of estimated pouch users in covered ZIPs |
| **Month 9–12** | ≥1 paying B2B customer ≥$500/mo; data accuracy ≥92% in audits | B2B buyers want different data (e.g., wholesale, promotions) → follow the buyer | Zero revenue after 12 months, **or** any unresolved legal complaint about collection methods |
| **Month 18** (before Path C) | Chicago cash-positive or ≥2 B2B customers; a buyer asking for more metros | — | Chicago still unprofitable with no expansion demand from buyers → don't expand |

**Recalibration of the business doc's gates:** G3's "≥500 MAU organic" and the hard kill "MAU < 2,000 after full-city coverage" are close to the *ceiling* for a city-only site (Memo 08: ~1,150 at 2.5% penetration). Replace them with **penetration of estimated users in covered areas (≥1.5%)** and **B2B revenue**, which better reflect what the business can become.

## 5. Shutdown plan (if the stop criteria trigger)
Low-cost and orderly: freeze data collection; keep the static tax-explainer content if it earns traffic (or sell the domain and content on a website marketplace); offer the historical dataset to researchers or buyers (it has value as a time series); dissolve the LLC (Illinois articles of dissolution) after final returns; delete personal data per the privacy policy.
